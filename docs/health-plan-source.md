# Personal Health & Knee OA Plan (Source)

## App concept

A personal-first health companion for daily weight management, knee osteoarthritis support, hydration, movement breaks, posture/eye breaks, exercise, food tracking, sleep, and progress.

The app should be designed for personal use first, with configurable routines so it can later support other users.

> **Medical note:** This is a lifestyle-management plan, not a diagnosis or replacement for an orthopedist/physiotherapist/dietitian. For knee OA, exercise should be progressed according to symptoms. Stop an exercise that causes sharp pain, significant swelling, locking, giving-way, or pain that remains substantially worse the next day, and seek professional advice when those occur.

# 1. Personal baseline

- Age: **41**
- Height: **5 ft 5 in / ~165 cm**
- Weight: **72 kg**
- Knee OA: **Left knee**
- Initial target: **65–67 kg**
- Weight-loss target: **5–7 kg**
- Practical target rate: **~0.25–0.5 kg/week**
- Initial planning horizon: **4 months**
- Main goals:
  1. Reduce body weight gradually.
  2. Improve leg and hip strength.
  3. Maintain knee mobility.
  4. Reduce prolonged sitting.
  5. Stay hydrated.
  6. Protect eyes and neck during software work.
  7. Build routines that are sustainable rather than extreme.

## Important correction to the earlier plan

Do **not** force a target of 0.4–0.6 kg every week. Weight loss is not linear. A slower rate is completely acceptable, especially when the priority is maintaining muscle and making the routine sustainable.

# 4. Software-engineer movement system

Long uninterrupted sitting is the bigger problem than simply failing to exercise once per day.

## A. Eye / distance break

Every **20–30 minutes**:

- Stop looking at the monitor.
- Look at a distant object for about **20 seconds**.
- Blink normally several times.

Example:
- Look through a window at a distant building/tree.
- Look down a hallway or across the room.

This should be a lightweight notification, not a disruptive alarm.

## B. Movement break

Every **45–60 minutes**:

Get away from the desk for **3–5 minutes**.

Do 2–3 of these:

- Walk around the room.
- Stand and gently move the knee.
- 10 calf raises.
- 5–10 controlled sit-to-stands if comfortable.
- Shoulder rolls.

Do not turn every break into a hard workout.

## C. Knee mobility break

Approximately every **2 hours**:

Spend **3–5 minutes** on gentle mobility.

Example:

1. Seated knee extension — 10 reps each leg.
2. Heel slides — 10 reps.
3. Ankle pumps — 20 reps.
4. Gentle calf stretch — 20 seconds × 2.

If a movement increases knee pain, reduce range/repetitions or skip it.

## D. Neck/posture break

Every **60–90 minutes**, spend about **3–5 minutes**:

1. Chin tucks — 8–10 reps.
2. Slow neck rotation — 5 each side.
3. Ear toward shoulder — 5 each side.
4. Shoulder rolls — 10 reps.
5. Chest-opening stretch — 20 seconds × 2.

Never force the neck into a stretch or make fast circular movements.

## E. Dhyana (meditation) break

Once or twice daily, distinct from the movement/eye/posture breaks above — a stillness practice rather than a movement one. See [dhyana-plan.md](dhyana-plan.md) for the full module design.

# 6. Strength / knee-support exercise plan

Strength training is important for OA management. The goal is to improve quadriceps, glutes, and general lower-body capacity without repeatedly irritating the knee.

Perform the main strength session **3 days/week**, for example Monday, Wednesday, Friday.

## Exercise 1 — Straight Leg Raise

- Lie on your back.
- Keep one leg bent and the exercising leg straight.
- Tighten the thigh.
- Raise the straight leg slowly.
- Lower slowly.

**Start:** 2 sets × 8–10 reps.

## Exercise 2 — Quad Set

- Sit or lie with the leg straight.
- Tighten the front thigh.
- Gently press the back of the knee toward the surface.
- Hold about 5 seconds.

**Start:** 2 sets × 10–15 reps.

## Exercise 3 — Sit-to-Stand

- Use a stable chair.
- Feet approximately hip-width apart.
- Stand up under control.
- Sit down slowly.

**Start:** 2 sets × 8–10 reps.

If this causes knee pain, use a higher chair and reduce the range.

## Exercise 4 — Glute Bridge

- Lie on your back.
- Bend the knees comfortably.
- Lift the hips.
- Pause briefly.
- Lower slowly.

**Start:** 2 sets × 10–12 reps.

## Exercise 5 — Calf Raise

- Hold a stable surface.
- Rise onto the balls of the feet.
- Lower slowly.

**Start:** 2 sets × 10–15 reps.

## Exercise 6 — Hip Abduction

- Hold a stable support.
- Move one leg sideways without leaning your body.
- Return slowly.

**Start:** 2 sets × 10 reps per side.

### Progression

When an exercise becomes easy and causes no meaningful symptom increase:

- First add repetitions.
- Then add a set.
- Then consider light resistance.

Do not chase exhaustion.

# 8. Pain-monitoring rule

The app should record:

- Pain before exercise: 0–10
- Pain during exercise: 0–10
- Pain 1–2 hours later
- Pain the next morning
- Swelling: yes/no
- Stiffness: 0–10

### Practical rule

A small, temporary increase in discomfort can occur with exercise. However:

- Sharp pain = stop.
- Significant swelling = reduce activity and consider professional assessment.
- Locking or giving-way = seek medical evaluation.
- Symptoms substantially worse the following day = reduce the previous session.

The app should **not** automatically tell the user to "push through pain."

# 14. Foods to reduce

There is no need for a "zero-carb" or "OA miracle food" diet.

Reduce:

- Sugary soft drinks
- Fruit juice
- Sweets
- Bakery foods
- Deep-fried snacks
- Frequent fast food
- Large portions of rice
- Frequent alcohol, if applicable

Foods such as biryani, pizza, sweets, or ice cream can fit occasionally. The problem is frequency and portion size, not one meal.

---

# 15. Protein target

For weight loss while preserving muscle, a useful planning range is approximately **80–100 g protein/day**, assuming no medical reason to restrict protein.

Spread protein across meals instead of trying to consume it all at dinner.

Examples:

- Breakfast: eggs + dairy
- Lunch: dal + chicken/fish/paneer
- Snack: curd/roasted chana
- Dinner: dal + fish/chicken/paneer/tofu

If you have kidney disease or have been advised to restrict protein, follow your clinician's advice instead.

# 16. Weight tracking

Do not judge progress from one day's weight.

### App routine

Weigh yourself:

- 3–7 mornings/week if comfortable.
- After using the bathroom.
- Before breakfast.
- Similar clothing/no clothing.
- Use the **weekly average**.

Track:

- Weight
- Waist circumference
- Knee pain
- Walking duration
- Daily steps
- Strength-session completion
- Sleep
- Hydration

### Goal

Aim for a gradual downward trend.

If weight has not moved for **3–4 weeks**, review food portions, snacks, drinks, activity, and adherence before making another change.

# 17. Four-month roadmap

## Month 1 — Build the habit

Target:
- Establish hydration reminders.
- Break long sitting periods.
- Walk regularly.
- Complete 3 strength sessions/week.
- Learn the exercises correctly.
- Begin food tracking.
- Do not chase rapid weight loss.

## Month 2 — Increase capacity

Target:
- 25–35 minute walks as tolerated.
- Progress strength exercises.
- Maintain hydration.
- Improve protein and vegetable intake.
- Continue weight trend monitoring.

## Month 3 — Consolidate

Target:
- 30–45 minute comfortable walks where tolerated.
- 3 strength sessions/week.
- Consistent sleep.
- Reduce unnecessary calorie-dense snacks.
- Continue monitoring knee response.

## Month 4 — Reach and maintain

Potential target:
- Around **65–67 kg**, but do not force this if your body is progressing more slowly.
- Shift focus from "losing weight" to maintaining the new weight.
- Continue strength training and regular walking.

# 22. Safety boundaries

The app should never present itself as diagnosing or treating OA.

Avoid statements such as:

> "This exercise will cure your arthritis."

Prefer:

> "This exercise may help improve strength and function. Perform it within a comfortable range."

The app should recommend professional assessment for:

- Significant swelling
- Knee locking
- Repeated giving-way
- New injury
- Severe or rapidly worsening pain
- Inability to bear weight
- Persistent symptoms despite an appropriate exercise program

---

# 23. Core principle

The app should optimize for **consistency**, not perfection.

A successful day is not:

> Perfect diet + 10,000 steps + hard workout.

A successful day is:

> Hydrated + moved regularly + completed appropriate exercise + sensible meals + adequate sleep + a few quiet minutes of dhyana.

That is the behavior the app should reinforce.
